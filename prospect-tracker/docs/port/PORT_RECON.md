# Port reconnaissance: git.eephus.io (2026-10-22)

**Who this is for:** the site owner and the webmaster.

**How it was done:** the remote was fetched read-only into `refs/eephusgit/*`. Nothing was pushed or committed, and no remote was configured.

**Where the paths point:**
- "Remote" means `scratchpad/eephusgit/main` (main = main-deploy).
- "Local" means `C:/Users/noah/baseball-projects/BaseballR/prospect-tracker`.

**The owner's goal (2026-10-22, binding):** "The goal is to have the public site (via git.eephus) be identical to the final local version of the site, while building on what the webmaster has already built in regards to the rebuild."

**How this report reads it:** as two hard requirements.
1. **IDENTICAL.** Every page, feature and served number on eephus.io equals the frozen local build. Acceptance is an equivalence check of every served page and data file, with each difference named and explained.
2. **BUILD ON HIS WORK.** His Go backend, deploy, Forgejo flow, frontend fixes and conventions are kept and extended, not bypassed.

## In short

git.eephus.io is not an empty remote waiting for a seed. It holds the webmaster's working replacement stack, and that stack is what eephus.io serves today. It has four parts:
- a Go nightly Build into SQLite,
- a Go `/partials/` server,
- a Go page generator,
- systemd publish units.

It serves a fork of our pages from 2026-07-19. Every page, feature and number the owner wants is still produced only by our local Python pipeline.

**The old runbook no longer works.** Its core move was to push 855 MiB of history to a new branch, run Python on the box and remove the partials. That now fails both requirements and several measured limits:
- the Cloudflare 100 MB push cap,
- his "No New Python Code" rule,
- a 55 GB disk,
- his PR flow.

**Recommendation: Strategy D, staged producer integration.**
- Our pipeline stays the one producer, outside his repo. It ships a frozen, sha256-manifested bundle.
- Small PRs into his `main` extend his own publish so the bundle goes live byte for byte.
- Acceptance is our `port_equivalence` check against the published site.
- His Go models stay as shadow challengers. Each one takes over a module only after a registered equivalence gate.

### Recommendation: D

**Stage 0: hygiene**
- A PR stops serving the six withheld files: `git rm` them from `frontend/data` and union `data_exclude.txt` with our 42 names.
- The webmaster first commits the box's `-dirty` diff as its own PR, so the deployed tree equals a commit.

**Stage 1: identical**

Our pipeline stays on the producer host and builds a frozen bundle: pages, assets, every payload and a sha256 `MANIFEST.tsv`. PRs into his `main` add:
- a committed `artifact_manifest.json` pin;
- our pages and assets, with his UX fixes re-applied;
- `publish_paths.txt` gaining the 15 page dirs plus history and parks, and dropping `teams`;
- a `publish.sh` filter that ships the served `/data/<subdir>/` trees from the bundle;
- an ownership skip-list in buildsite, so it never overwrites pages the bundle owns;
- a receive unit that verifies the manifest and swaps a `current` symlink atomically, in the same pattern as his `Current`.

Two gates apply:
- **Gate C:** `port_equivalence` compares the frozen local snapshot (REF) with a staging webroot, and then with production, over HTTP. Every difference is named.
- **Gate B:** all 42 withheld names return 404.

Release goes `main` -> `main-deploy` by his PR.

**Stage 2: per route**
- A Go artifact-import Source loads the bundle into `nightly.db`.
- A JSON sidecar route must return bytes equal to the bundle file.
- An HTML fragment route is re-templated. Its page goes back to htmx only when the rendered page and its numbers equal the static page.

**Stage 3: per model**
- A Go re-implementation (consensus, xFV, valuation, projections, plus boards) replaces the Python producer for one module.
- It may do so only after a pre-registered equivalence gate against the local numbers.
- The as-of accessor must be in his schema first (PORT_SPEC 4.1).

**How each requirement is met**
- **IDENTICAL:** at Stage 1 the bytes served are the local build's, proven by `port_equivalence`. Expected named differences:
  - headshot URLs moving to his `/images/headshots` proxy, if the owner accepts that;
  - the forums and accounts layer;
  - the script Cloudflare injects.
- **BUILD ON HIS WORK:** everything goes through his repo, his PR review, his `publish.sh` and `go test` gate, his Cloudflare purge, heartbeat, headshot cache and systemd units. His partials are brought to equivalence instead of being dropped. His Go models become the post-port backend program (PORT_SPEC 5.7 lanes 5-6), each behind a gate.
- **Standing rule:** "never port and rewrite at once" holds (PORT_SPEC 5.1).
- **What D avoids:** no .py file enters his repo, no 855 MiB history push, no 29.7 GB cache on the box.

## 8. Risks

- **Producer on the owner's PC.** The PC blue-screens under load, and a missed night leaves the site stale. The 36-hour heartbeat alarm must watch the bundle receive time, not his Go Build time.
- **Two writers.** His 03:10 Build followed by `publish.sh` (`rsync --delete`) overwrites the webroot. The bundle must publish through that same `publish.sh`, or the site flips between his numbers and ours every night.
- **buildsite.** Without the skip-list, it rewrites the 13 shell pages to July markup and his numbers every night.
- **Data subdirectories.** About 22 local subdirectories would 404 under today's filter (pitch_plots alone is about 1 GB). His `go test` gate (`pages_test.go:17`, TestSitemapSurfacesAreShipped) blocks publish on any manifest mismatch.
- **Live jobs.** Betting odds every 15 minutes, the umpire job and scores are Python jobs. Under producer (a) they run on the PC, or the betting slate goes stale.
- **Cloudflare caching.** `/data/*.json` has a 4-hour edge TTL and `/assets/` a 1-year one. A missed purge, or an asset whose `?v=` stamp does not change, serves stale or mixed versions.
- **Disk.** The box was recorded at 93% full. Two bundles (about 3.5 GB) plus his 6.5 GB baseline must stay above the 6 GB `EEPHUS_DISK_MIN_GB` floor.
- **Review latency.** He is the only reviewer for about 10 Stage 1 PRs. Stage 2 (20 fragment routes) may stall, leaving static pages beside unused partials for a long time.
- **Different numbers.** His Build is a different source: bWAR, summer xFV and a slug key. Any route that serves from his tables before Stage 3 serves different numbers. LV-10 measured 25 of 80 fields equal.
- **Leakage.** Stages 2-3 add request-time queries over a store with no as-of column. The accessor has to be an entry condition, and his repo has to keep that promise.
- **Visible changes.** The forums and accounts layer and the headshot proxy must be accepted as named differences, or the identical check cannot pass.
