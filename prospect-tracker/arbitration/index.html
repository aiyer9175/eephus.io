Losses

On the same footing as the wins
  • our SERVED estimator against the PUBLISHED pre-settlement Swartz projection, on settled figures, identical rows: This model does not beat the MLB Trade Rumors / Matt Swartz projection. Measured on identical rows, Swartz is materially more accurate at every arbitration step. Reported as a NULL. The mechanism is not mysterious: Swartz prices the counting stats arbitration panels actually reward - home runs, RBI, saves, wins - and this champion deliberately excludes them because nothing in this pipeline projects them forward, which is what lets the same model serve a backtest and a forecast. Swartz is the better estimate of a KNOWN season; it is not available for an unplayed one.
  • the model beats the step-multiplier baseline on arb 2/3/4: The arb-2-and-later model is a NULL. Its margin over a median step multiplier off the prior salary is below the floor measured for this basis. The A3 control shows the production block does carry signal there - permuting it makes the model WORSE than the baseline - so this is a power limit, not an absence of information, and it is still reported as a null.

What this model cannot tell you

  • It prices the SYSTEM, not the hearing. There is no access to filed figures, so a case that went to a panel is scored on its award alone.
  • Arbitration steps before 2022 are reconstructed, and the substitute sources measure 0.66 and 0.70 agreement against published service time. Those seasons are reported and NOT scored.
  • 2020 is never a label year. Salaries were paid at 60/162 of contract and the sources disagree on which basis they report.
  • The collective bargaining agreement in force expires 2026-12-01. Every projected season from 2027 assumes its arbitration structure survives unchanged. That is not estimated from data and no interval here covers it.
  • Non-tendered players are absent by construction. They are the bottom of the award distribution, so summing these projections OVERSTATES what a club actually owes.
