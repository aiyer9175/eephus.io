The 20-80 scale

50 is major league average, and each 10 points is one standard deviation from it (McDaniel 2014; Longenhagen and McDaniel 2018).

Three deviations each way hold 99.7% of a normal population, so the scale runs from 20 to 80 (McDaniel 2014).

The population is major league players, not all of baseball (Longenhagen 2022).

Both ends are censored: an 80 means three deviations above average or more, and a 20 means three below or less (Longenhagen 2022).

At three deviations, an 80 is about 1 player in 1,000; about 750 players open a season on major league rosters (Smith 2013).

Scouts grade in 45 and 55 around average, where players cluster; 35 and 65 come mostly from analysts reading measured data (Longenhagen and McDaniel 2018).

Where 50 sits depends on the population counted: a league-average hitter over 550 plate appearances is worth 2.0 WAR (Cistulli 2013).

FanGraphs defines the scale by standard deviations; Baseball America and MLB Pipeline publish adjectives without that claim (Glaser; MLB.com glossary).

Some clubs grade on a 2-8 scale with no half grades, and all three outlets say so.

A blank cell is blank in the source. FanGraphs names no grade below 45, and MLB Pipeline bands 20 to 30 and 70 to 80.

Source: McDaniel 2014, Glaser, MLB.com glossary

Each tool carries a present and a future grade, written present/future: 20/50 is a present 20 and a future 50 (McDaniel 2014).

"Hit: 30/45, Game Power: 60/70, Raw Power: 80/80, Speed: 40/40, Field: 45/50, Throw: 70/70"

McDaniel 2014, on Joey Gallo: McDaniel 2014

FV, Future Value, is one 20-80 grade for the whole player, with risk and distance from the majors inside it (Longenhagen and McDaniel 2018).

Hitters carry five tools: hit, power, run, field and throw. Pitchers carry a grade per pitch plus command (McDaniel 2014).

Power splits into game power, home runs per full season at maturity, and raw power, the deepest batting-practice shot (McDaniel 2014).

WAR by FV tier

FanGraphs maps FV to average annual WAR over a player's first six major league seasons, not a peak season (Longenhagen and McDaniel 2018).

'If he arrives' is WAR for a player who reaches his projected role; 'Expected, bust-inclusive' averages every prospect at the grade, busts at zero. Reading one as the other overprices a 50 FV about two times and a 40 or 45 FV about three times (Longenhagen and McDaniel 2018; Clemens 2026).

Our prime-season table gives WAR in a full prime season for a player who sticks, from FV 35 up. The FanGraphs primers read FV as a six-season average, and a player's peak season grades one to two grades above that (Longenhagen 2022).

45 and up: the 2014-2020 average (3,463 players), blended half and half with the public Edwards scale, more public weight where a tier is thin, the top tier the public scale alone; 35 and 40: re-measured on FanGraphs WAR without a blend; below 35: a floor, not a measurement.

This column is the table our valuations use today. Its registered calibration test, the Prospect Anchor gate, read it once on 2026-10-14 and could not decide it, which is not a confirmation. The question opens again once the 2022 debut cohort resolves.

The glossary's methodology table prints the configuration's starting values for this table until the Prospect Anchor gate moves that table to what serves; this page prints what serves.

Expected WAR is the total, undiscounted WAR over team control, averaged over every prospect at the grade, including those who never reached the majors.

A star posts two or more seasons of 4+ WAR in team control. The table has no 75 or 80 row (Clemens 2026).

Our column is per season and this one is a team-control total, so the two sit on different scales.

How eephus uses grades

Consensus FV and tool grades print rounded up to the whole grade: 62.1 prints as 63.

eFV, our model grade, rounds to the nearest whole grade, as do plus stats.

For hit, game power, pitch selection, bat control, fastball and command, PROSPX shows the eTool: the consensus grade moved by the prospect's own minor-league stats. It is a model grade, so it rounds to the nearest whole grade and carries an e; every other tool shows the consensus grade, which carries a c.

Our consensus maps MLB Pipeline and Baseball America grades onto the FanGraphs scale before combining sources.

Definitions live in the Library, player grades on PROSPX, and Source Accuracy compares each outlet's grades by cohort.
